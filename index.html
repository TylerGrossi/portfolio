Puck Engine: NHL advanced stats

A public NHL analytics site where every advanced stat is computed in house from the league's raw play-by-play and shift data, instead of being bought from third-party providers.

What I built

  • Expected-goals model (LightGBM) that rates the scoring chance of every shot from distance, angle, shot type, rebounds and game state. The model is exported to JSON and scored in plain JavaScript, so live games are scored on the server without Python.
  • Wins Above Replacement (WAR) built from shift charts with regularized adjusted plus-minus (sparse ridge regression). It replaced two third-party data sources.
  • Multi-year player projections that blend weighted history with k-nearest-neighbor comparable players. 15 candidate models were compared in the backtest.
  • Trade-value model fitted to 1,547 real NHL trades. It powers an "Armchair GM" tool that suggests fair trades under the salary cap.
  • Cut first page load from 2.14 MB to 0.78 MB (−64%) through lazy loading and caching.

FBB Sim: fantasy basketball simulator

A simulation and decision platform for a 15-category ESPN fantasy basketball league. It turns live league data into win probabilities, playoff odds and recommended roster moves.

What I built

  • Probabilistic matchup model: each category is projected as a distribution, and correlated outcomes are drawn with a Gaussian copula (10,000 draws). It runs in the browser in under 1 ms, so every what-if updates instantly.
  • Model parameters estimated from a full season of box scores instead of hand-tuned guesses. One example: free-throw variance had been set about 3× too low.
  • Monte Carlo season and playoff simulator that accounts for injuries. Drawing each player's total in closed form made it ~40× faster (385 ms → 10 ms) with an identical distribution.
  • Week-long add/drop optimizer (greedy search plus local search), validated against exhaustive brute-force search.
  • Injury availability models (calibrated gradient boosting) trained on 79,697 official NBA injury-report listings. I caught and removed a label leak that had inflated an early AUC to 0.988.

Earnings Momentum Model

A trading strategy that buys stocks showing positive momentum during the week they report earnings. It is automated end to end, from data collection through backtesting to placing (paper) trades.

What I built

  • Automated data pipeline: a GitHub Actions job runs twice a day. It scrapes momentum signals, validates earnings dates against a second source and tracks 1- to 90-day returns.
  • Realistic backtester using hourly prices that takes the actual loss when a stock gaps down past its stop, instead of assuming a perfect fill.
  • ML classifier (random forest / gradient boosting) that scores each new trade's probability of a positive 5-day return.
  • Broker integration: places market-on-close orders through the Alpaca API in a paper trading account.
  • Streamlit app with a live scanner, stop-loss analysis, an embedded Power BI risk report, a mean-variance optimizer and an AI assistant.

Machine learning · ETLPower BI

Movie rating predictor

A pipeline that learns my taste from 312 films I've rated, then predicts my score for each of the 762 films on my watchlist. The results feed an 11-page Power BI report.

What I built

  • ETL: exports my IMDb ratings, then enriches every film through the OMDb and TMDB APIs, with incremental caching and handling for rate limits.
  • Found and fixed target leakage: features built from my own scores were leaking the answer into training. The original model's cross-validated R² of 0.803 dropped to −0.12 once I measured it properly.
  • Rebuilt the model honestly: every score-based feature is recomputed inside each CV fold. It combines a critics baseline, personal taste deviations and collaborative filtering from the 32-million-rating MovieLens dataset.
  • On films I watched later (a time-based test), the final model beat the previous version on 5 of 5 folds.

NFL Big Data Bowl 2027

A Kaggle competition entry that uses player-tracking data to test whether how a receiver brakes and turns in Combine drills predicts his NFL production.

What I'm doing

  • Physics-based features: showed the provided acceleration field couldn't separate braking from turning, then rebuilt velocity and acceleration vectors from raw coordinates.
  • Reliability before validity: core metrics repeat at r = 0.96–0.98 across attempts.
  • Multiple-comparison control: screened 100 metric × outcome pairs with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. Braking showed no effect, and I report that null result rather than cherry-picking.
  • Bayesian errors-in-variables model: receivers who turn hard at speed earn more targets per route (+0.43 SD, 90% credible interval 0.21–0.65). The finding is now under a pre-registered test on the 2026 season.
